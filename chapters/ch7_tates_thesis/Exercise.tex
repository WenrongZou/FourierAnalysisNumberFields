\begin{exercise}[1]
  \label{ex:ch7-1}
  Let $F$ be a local field and let $\psi$ be a nontrivial unitary character on
  $F$; that is, a continuous, nontrivial homomorphism from $F$ as an additive
  group into $S^1$, the circle group. For each $a \in F$, define
  $\psi_a \colon F \to S^1$ by
  \[
    \psi_a(x) = \psi(ax).
  \]
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Show that $\psi_a$ is again a unitary character of $F$, and that
      $\psi_a$ is trivial if and only if $a = 0$.
    % The book writes "\psi \mapsto \psi_a" (typo).
    \item Show that $a \mapsto \psi_a$ defines a continuous, injective
      homomorphism $\alpha_\psi$ of $F$ into its Pontryagin dual $\dual{F}$.
    % The book writes "for all b \in F" (typo).
    \item Show that the image of $\alpha_\psi$ is dense in $\dual{F}$.
      [\emph{Hint}: Show that $\psi_a(b) = 1$ for all $a \in F$ if and only if
      $b = 0$.]
    \item Show that $\alpha_\psi$ is bicontinuous.
    \item Show that $\alpha_\psi(F)$ is a complete, hence closed, subgroup of
      $\dual{F}$. Conclude that $\alpha_\psi$ is an isomorphism of topological
      groups.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
(a) Multiplication by $a$, $x \mapsto ax$, is a continuous endomorphism of the
additive group $F$. Hence $\psi_a$, its composite with $\psi$, is a continuous
homomorphism $F \to S^1$, i.e.\ a unitary character of $F$.

If $a = 0$, then $\psi_a(x) = \psi(0) = 1$ for all $x \in F$, so $\psi_a$ is
trivial. Conversely, let $a \neq 0$. Since $\psi$ is nontrivial, there is some
$y \in F$ with $\psi(y) \neq 1$, and then $\psi_a(a^{-1}y) = \psi(y) \neq 1$.
So $\psi_a$ is nontrivial.

(b) Recall that $\dual{F}$ has the compact-open topology. A basis of neighborhoods of a
character $\psi_0$ is given by the sets
\[W(\psi_0, C, \varepsilon) = \{\psi \in \dual{F}:  |\psi(x) - \psi_0(x)| < \varepsilon, \forall x \in C \}. \]
where $C\subset F$ is a compact set and $\varepsilon > 0$.

First, reduce to continuity at $0$. Note that $\psi_{a + b} (x)= \psi_a (x) \cdot \psi_b(x)$.
Then we have that
\[|\psi_a(x)- \psi_b(x)| = |\psi_b|\cdot |\psi_{a-b}(x) - 1 | = |\psi_{a-b}(x) - 1|. \]
So it suffices to show that for each compact $C$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, there is a $\delta >0$, such
that
% \[|a| < \delta \implies |\psi_a(x) - 1| < \varepsilon. \]
$\forall |a| < \delta$, $|\psi_a(x) - 1| < \varepsilon$.

Given an $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $\psi$ is continuous at $0$ and $\psi(0) = 1$, then there is an $r > 0$, such that
$\forall |x| < r$, $|\psi(x) - 1|< \varepsilon$. Since $C$ is compact, then $M = \max_{x \in C} \{ |x|\} < \infty$.
Choose $\delta = r / M$, then $\forall |a| < \delta$ and $\forall x \in C$, $|a x| < r$.
It follows that
\[|\psi_a(x) - 1| = |\psi(ax) - 1| < \varepsilon. \]

Injectivity is followed from (a).

(c)
Note that
\[\forall a \in F, \psi_a(b) = 1 \iff \forall a \in F, \psi(a b) = 1 \iff b = 0. \]

Recall that we have the fact of Pontryagin dual: let $G$ be a locally compact abelian group
and $H$ be a proper closed subgroup of $G$. Then there is a nontrivial character $\chi$ of $G$, such that
$\chi|_H = 1$.

Let $H$ be the closure of the image of $\alpha_\psi$, i.e. $H = \overline{\alpha_\psi(F)}$.
Assume that $H \neq \dual F$. Then we have a character $\chi \in \dual{\dual F}$ such that
$\chi$ is trivial on $H$. And by Pontryagin duality $F \equiv \dual{\dual{F}}$, every character
of $\dual F$ is evaluation at some point $b \in F$, i.e. $\psi \mapsto \psi (b)$.
Then we have that $\chi = (\psi \mapsto \psi(b_0))$ for some $b_0 \in F$. Since
$\chi$ is trivial on $H$ then $\forall a \in F, \psi_a(b_0) = 1$. It follows that
$b_0 = 0$, which contradicts to $\chi$ is a nontrivial character of $\dual{F}$.

(d) First, reduce to continuity at $0 \in F$. Note that $\psi_a \in \psi_{a_0} W(1, C, \varepsilon)$ if and only if
$\psi_{a - a_0} \in W(1, C, \varepsilon)$. Then it suffices to show that $\forall r > 0$, there are a
compact $C$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, such that
\[\psi_a\in W(1, C, \varepsilon) \implies |a|< r. \]

Since $\psi$ is nontrivial, pick $y \in F$ such that $\psi(y) \neq 1$, and set
$\varepsilon = |\psi(y) - 1| > 0$. Now choose $C \coloneqq \{x \in F: |x| \le |y|/r \}$.
Since $C$ is closed, then $C$ is compact.

Now suppose that $\psi_a \in W(1, C, \varepsilon)$ but $|a| \ge r$. Then $a \neq 0$ and
$x \coloneqq a^{-1} y$ satisfies $|x| = |y|/ |a| \le |y| / r$, so $x \in C$. But
\[|\psi_a(x) - 1| = |\psi(y) - 1| = \varepsilon, \]
which contradicts to $\psi_a\in W(1, C, \varepsilon)$. Therefore, $|a| < r$.

(e) Given a Cauchy sequence $\{\psi_{a_n}\}_n$ in $\alpha_\psi(F)$. Then we have that
for every neighborhood $W$ of $1$ there is an $N$ such that $\psi_{a_n - a_m} \in W$
for all $n, m \ge N$. By (d), there is a neighborhood $W$ such that
\[\psi_b \in W \implies |b| < r. \]

Then we have that $\{a_n\}_n$ is a Cauchy sequence in $F$. Since $F$ is complete,
then $\{a_n\}_n$ converges uniquely to $a \in F$. By (b), we have that $\psi_{a_n}$ converges
to $\psi_a$. Since $\dual{F}$ is Hausdorff, the limit is unique.
Therefore, $\alpha_{\psi} (F)$ is complete. And since every complete subspace of a Hausdorff space is
closed, then $\alpha_{\psi} (F)$ is closed. In conclusion, $\alpha_\psi$ is an isomorphism
of topological groups.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[2]
  \label{ex:ch7-2}
  Let $F$ be a non-Archimedean local field with ring of integers $\gotho_F$
  and maximal ideal $P$. Fix a nontrivial unitary character $\psi$ of $F$.
  Let $P^m$ be the conductor of $\psi$; that is, the largest fractional ideal
  $P^m$ on which $\psi$ is trivial. Let $\alpha_\psi$, as defined in
  \cref{ex:ch7-1}, be the isomorphism between $F$ and its Pontryagin dual.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Show that via $\alpha_\psi$, $P^m$ identifies with the Pontryagin
      dual of $\dual{\gotho}_F$. (This is also the inverse different of $F$,
      which soon makes its appearance.)
    \item Let $n \ge 1$ and $x \in F$. Show that $xP^n \subseteq \ker(\psi)$
      if and only if $x \in P^{m-n}$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
  (a) Claim: $\alpha(P^m)$ is the annilator of $\gotho_F$ in $\dual{F}$, i.e.
    \[\alpha(P^m) = \gotho_F^\perp \coloneqq \{\chi \in \dual{F} : \chi|_{\gotho_F} = 1\}. \]
  In fact, for every $x \in F$,
  \[\psi_x \in \gotho_F^\perp \iff \psi(x \gotho_F) = 1 \iff x\gotho_F \subseteq \ker \psi
    \iff x\gotho_F \subseteq P^m \iff x \in P^m. \]
  (b) Note that
  \[ xP^n \subseteq \ker (\psi) \iff x P^n \subseteq P ^ m \iff v(x) + n \ge m \iff x \in P^{m - n}. \]
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[3]
  \label{ex:ch7-3}
  In this exercise we construct nontrivial characters for non-Archimedean
  local fields, including the standard character for $\QQ_p$ and its
  extensions, and the standard character for the completion of $\FF_p(t)$ and
  its extensions, at the place defined by $t^{-1}$.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Given any $x \in \QQ_p$, let $n$ be the smallest nonnegative integer
      such that $p^n x$ lies in $\ZZ_p$. Let $r \in \ZZ$ be such that
      $r \equiv p^n x \pmod{p^n}$. Put
      \[
        \psi(x) = e^{2\pi i r/p^n}.
      \]
      Show that $\psi \colon \QQ_p \to S^1$ is a nontrivial unitary character
      of conductor $\ZZ_p$ and that it identifies with the composite map
      $\QQ_p \to \QQ_p/\ZZ_p \to \QQ/\ZZ \to S^1$.
    % Changed from the book: the book has \psi_\infty(x) = e^{2\pi i a_1/p}
    % (a_1 the coefficient of t) with conductor F_p[[t^{-1}]]. Then
    % Exercise 5(d) fails for either sign: every y there lies in
    % t^{-1}F_p[[t^{-1}]], so a_1(y) = 0 (e.g. \pi = t, y = 1/t gives
    % \psi_v(y)\psi_\infty(y) = e^{2\pi i/p}). We use instead the character
    % compatible with Exercises 5 and 6 (the residue of x dt at infinity),
    % x |-> e^{-2\pi i a_{-1}/p}, whose conductor is t^{-2} F_p[[t^{-1}]].
    \item Let $K_\infty$ denote the completion of $K = \FF_p(t)$ defined by
      the place $t^{-1}$. Show that $K_\infty = \FF_p\lseries{t^{-1}}$; that is,
      that each $x \in K_\infty$ can be uniquely represented by a formal power
      series
      \[
        \sum_{n=-\infty}^{r} a_n t^n
      \]
      with $a_n \in \ZZ/p\ZZ$ for all $n$ less than or equal to the integer
      $r$. Put
      \[
        \psi_\infty(x) = e^{-2\pi i a_{-1}/p} \in S^1.
      \]
      Show that $\psi_\infty$ is a nontrivial unitary character of $K_\infty$
      of conductor $t^{-2}\FF_p\pseries{t^{-1}}$. (\emph{Note}: This differs
      from the book, which puts $\psi_\infty(x) = e^{2\pi i a_1/p}$, of
      conductor $\FF_p\pseries{t^{-1}}$; with that choice part~(d) of
      \cref{ex:ch7-5} fails.)
    \item Let $\pi$ be any irreducible polynomial in $\FF_p[t] \subseteq K$,
      and let $K_\pi$ denote the completion of $K$ defined by the prime ideal
      $(\pi)$, with residue field $k$, which we may identify with a
      transversal of $(\pi)$ in $\FF_p[t]$. Show that every $x \in K_\pi$ can
      be written in the form
      \[
        \sum_{n=r}^{+\infty} a_n \pi^n
      \]
      for some integer $r$, with each coefficient $a_n \in k$. Next put
      \[
        \psi_\pi(x) = e^{2\pi i \tr_{k/\FF_p}(a_{-1})/p}.
      \]
      Show that $\psi_\pi$ is a nontrivial unitary character of $K_\pi$.
    \item Let $F$ be any non-Archimedean local field. Show that $F$ is a
      finite separable extension of a local field $F_0$ as in parts~(a), (b),
      or~(c) above. Let $\psi_0$ denote the corresponding character of $F_0$
      defined therein. Given $x \in F$, set
      \[
        \psi(x) = \psi_0(\tr_{F/F_0}(x)).
      \]
      Show that $\psi$ is a nontrivial unitary character of $F$.
    \item Continuing in the same context, show that when the characteristic
      of $F$ is zero, the conductor of $\psi$ is the inverse different
      $\calD_F^{-1}$. In this case show also that for $f$ the characteristic
      function of $\gotho_F$, the Fourier transform of $f$ is
      $N(\calD_F)^{-1/2}$ times the characteristic function of
      $\calD_F^{-1}$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
(a) It is easy to see that $\psi$ is continuous and $|\psi(x)| = 1$.
Note that
\[\forall x \in \ZZ_p, \ \psi(x) = e ^ {2 \pi i r} = 1, i.e. \ \psi|_{\ZZ_p} = 1 \]
And since $\psi (1 / p) = e^{2 \pi i/ p} \neq 1$, then $\ZZ_p$ is the largest fractional ideal
that $\psi$ is trivial.

For any $x_1, x_2\in \QQ_p$, we are going to show $\psi(x_1 + x_2) = \psi(x_1) \psi(x_2)$.
Let $n_i$ be the smallest nonnegative integer such that $p^{n_i} x_i$ lies in $\ZZ_p$, and let
$r_i \in \ZZ$ be such that $r_i \equiv p^{n_i} x_i \pmod{p^{n_i}}$. WLOG, we can assume that
$0 < n_1 < n_2$. Then $n_2$ is the smallest integer such that $p^{n_2} (x + y) \in \ZZ_p$,
and $p^{n_2}(x + y) \equiv p^{n_2 - n_1} p^{n_1} x + p^ {n_2} y \equiv p^{n_2 - n_1} r_1 + r_2
\pmod{p ^ {n_2}}$. Therefore,
\[\psi(x + y) = e^{2\pi i (p^{n_2 - n_1} r_1 + r_2) / p^{n_2}} =  e^{2\pi i r_1/ p^{n_1}} e^{2 \pi i r_2/ p^{n_2}}
= \psi(x) \psi(y).  \]
It follows that $\psi$ is a nontrivial unitary character of conductor $\ZZ_p$.
And it identisfied with the composite map $\QQ_p \to \QQ_p / \ZZ_p \to \QQ/ \ZZ \to S^1$ as follows:
\[x \mapsto p ^ n x \mapsto r / p ^ n \mapsto e ^ {2 \pi i r / p ^ n}. \]

(b) It is easy to see that $\psi_\infty (x + y) = \psi_\infty(x) \psi_\infty(y)$. And for
every $x \in t^{-2}\FF_p\pseries{t^{-1}}$, $a_{-1} = 0$, then $\psi_\infty(x) = e^{-2 \pi i a_{-1} / p} = 1$.
And for $t^{-1} \in t^{-1} \FF_p\pseries{t^{-1}}$, $\psi_\infty (t^{-1}) = e^{-2 \pi i / p} \neq 1$.
Since every fractional ideal strictly containing $t^{-2}\FF_p\pseries{t^{-1}}$ contains
$t^{-1}\FF_p\pseries{t^{-1}}$, the conductor of $\psi_\infty$ is $t^{-2}\FF_p\pseries{t^{-1}}$.
In particular, $\psi_\infty$ is trivial on an open subgroup, hence continuous.

(c) Write $A = \FF_p[t], d = \deg \pi, q = p ^ d$. Let $\gotho_\pi = \varprojlim A / (\pi^n)$ be the
completion of $A$ at $(\pi)$, $K_\pi = \gotho_\pi [1/ \pi]$ and the residue field $k = \gotho_\pi
/ \pi \gotho_\pi = A / (\pi) = \FF_q$. Take
\[S = \{f \in A : \deg f < d\}. \]
By division algorithm, we have a bijection between $S$ and $A / (\pi) = k$.

Next we want to show that there is a unique $\pi$-adic expansion. WLOG, we only consider
$x \in \gotho_\pi$. For general $x \neq 0$, take $r = v(x)$ and apply the result to
$y = \pi ^{-r} x \in \gotho_\pi$.

Since $S \to k$ is surjective, choose $a_0 \in S$ such that $x \equiv a_0 \pmod{\pi}$. Then take
$x_1 = (x - a_0)/ \pi$ and choose $a_1 \in S$ such that $x_1 \equiv a_1 \pmod{\pi}$. By
induction, we have
\[x - \sum_{i = 0}^{n-1} a_i \pi ^ i = \pi^n x_n. \]
Then $|x - \sum_{i = 0}^{n-1} a_i \pi ^ i| \le q ^ -n \to 0$.
Therefore, $x = \sum_{i\ge 0}a_i \pi ^i$. And the uniqueness follows from the irreducibility of
$\pi$.

Since $\tr_{k/\FF_p}(x + y) = \tr_{k/ \FF_p}(x) + \tr_{k/ \FF_p}(y)$, then
$\psi_\pi(x + y) = \psi_\pi(x) \psi_\pi(y)$.

(d) Homomorphism property follows from the fact that $\tr_{F/F_0}$ is additive. Since $\tr_{F/F_0}$
is $F_0$ linear on finite dimensional $F_0$-space, then $\tr_{F/F_0}$ is continuous.
Since $F/ F_0$ is separable extension, then $\tr_{F/F_0}$ is surjective, then $\psi(x)$ is nontrivial.

(e) Since the characteristic of $F$ is $0$, then we assume that $F_0= \QQ_p$.
Recall that the conductor $\psi_0$ is $\ZZ_p$ and
\[\calD_F ^{-1} = \{x \in F: \tr(x \gotho) \subseteq \ZZ_p\}.\]

Claim: For any fractional ideal $\gotha$, $\psi|_{\gotha} = 1$ if and only if $\gotha \subseteq \calD_F^{-1}$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of claim]
Note that
\[\psi|_\gotha = 1 \iff \psi_0|_{\tr(\gotha)} = 1 \iff \tr(\gotha) \subseteq \ZZ_p. \]
It remains to show that $\tr(\gotha) \subseteq \ZZ_p \iff \gotha \subseteq \calD_F^{-1}$.

($\impliedby$): For each $a \in \gotha \subseteq \calD_F^{-1}$, by definition of $\calD_F^{-1}$,
we have that $\tr(a) \in \ZZ_p$.

($\implies$): For each $a \in \gotha$, $a \gotho \subseteq \gotha$. It follows that
$\tr(a\gotho) \subseteq \ZZ_p$, i.e. $a\in \calD_F^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

Next, we will show that
\[\dual{\bbone_{\gotho_F}} = N(\calD_F)^{-1/2} \bbone_{\calD_F^{-1}}. \]

Recall that for $f \in S(F)$, $\dual{f}(x) = \int_{F} f(y) \psi(x y) \dd y$. And we have
the follow lemma:

\begin{lemma}
  Let $H$ be a compact open subgroup of $F$, $\mu$ a Haar measure on $F$, and $\chi$ a
  character of $H$. Then
  \[\int_{H} \chi \dd\mu =
    \begin{cases}
      \mu(H), & \text{if } \chi = 1, \\
      0, & \text{otherwise}.
    \end{cases}
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch of proof]
If $\chi$ is nontrivial, then there is a $y \in H$ such that
$\chi(y) \neq 1$, then $\int_{H} \chi (x) \dd\mu(x) =
\int_{H} \chi(xy) \dd \mu(x y) = \chi(y)\int_{H} \chi (x) \dd\mu(x) $.
Hence, $\int_{H} \chi (x) \dd\mu(x) = 0$.
\end{proof}

Let $\dd x$ be the Haar measure such that $\int_{\gotho} \dd x = 1$.
Note that $\psi(x y)$ is trivial on $\gotho$ if and only if $x \in \calD_F^{-1}$.
\begin{equation}
  \dual{\bbone_{\gotho_F}} (x) = \int_{\gotho} \psi(x y) \dd y
= \int_{\gotho} \psi_0(\tr(xy)) \dd y = \bbone_{\calD_F^{-1}}. \label{eq: ex3}
\end{equation}
% \[\dual{\bbone_{\gotho_F}} (x) = \int_{\gotho} \psi(x y) \dd y
% = \int_{\gotho} \psi_0(\tr(xy)) \dd y = \bbone_{\calD_F^{-1}}. \label{eq: ex3} \]

Assume that $c' \dd x$ be the dual measure of $\dd x$ under the transport $\alpha$.
Fourier inversion formula should holds for $f = \bbone_{\gotho}$, i.e.
$\dual{\dual{\bbone_{\gotho}}} = \bbone_{\gotho}$.
Note that
\[\dual{\dual{\bbone_{\gotho}}}(x) = \int_{F} \bbone_{\calD_F^{-1}}(y) \psi(-xy) c' \dd y =
c' \int_{\calD_{F} ^ {-1}} \psi(-xy) \dd y = c' \vol(\calD_F^{-1}, \dd x) \bbone_{\gotho}.  \]

And note that $\vol (\calD_F^{-1}, \dd x) = N(\calD_F)$. It follows that $c' = N(\calD_F)^{-1}$.

Now assume that for the self-dual has the form $t \dd x$, then the dual Haar measure,
transported by $\alpha$ is $t^{-1} c' \dd x = t^{-1} N(\calD_F) ^{-1} \dd x$. Then we have the
following equality:
\[t \dd x = t^{-1} N(\calD_F)^{-1} \dd x.\]

It follows that $t = N(\calD_F) ^{-1/2}$. Normalize the equation \eqref{eq: ex3},
we complete this exercise.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[4]
  \label{ex:ch7-4}
  We shall now construct the standard character on $\AAA_K/K$ for an
  algebraic number field $K$.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item For each rational prime $p$, let $\psi_p$ denote the standard
      character of $\QQ_p$ defined above. Also define
      $\psi_\infty \colon \RR \to S^1$ by $\psi_\infty(x) = e^{-2\pi i x}$.
      Show that
      \[
        \psi \colon \AAA_\QQ \to S^1, \qquad
        (x_v) \mapsto \prod_v \psi_v(x_v)
      \]
      is a nontrivial unitary character on $\AAA_\QQ$, which is trivial on
      $\QQ$ (embedded diagonally). [\emph{Hint}: Use the Artin product
      formula.]
    \item For any number field $K$, define $\psi_K \colon \AAA_K \to S^1$ by
      \[
        \psi_K(x) = \prod_v \psi_v(x_v)
      \]
      where $v$ ranges over all the places of $K$ and $\psi_v$ is the standard
      character on the local field $K_v$, as constructed above. Show that
      $\psi_K$ is a nontrivial unitary character on $\AAA_K$.
    \item Show also that $\psi_K(x) = \psi(\tr(x))$ where $\tr$ denotes the
      adelic trace map from $K$ to $\QQ$. Conclude from part~(a) that
      $\psi_K$ is trivial on $K$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
  (a) Recall that
  \begin{theorem}[Artin product formula]
    For a global field $K$, namely number field or function field of a curve over $\FF_q$, and
    any non-zero $x \in K$, we have that
    \[ \prod_{v} |x|_v = 1, \]
    where $v$ run over all places of $K$.
  \end{theorem}

  It is easy to check that $\psi(x + y) = \psi(x) \psi(y)$. Since
  for any place $v$, $|\psi_v(x_v)| = 1$, then $|\psi(x)| = 1$.
  Note that: a homomorphism of topological groups that is continuous
  on a open subgroup is continuous everywhere. It suffices to show that
  $\psi$ is continuous on some open subgroup $U$.
  Take $U = \RR \times \prod_{p} \ZZ_p$. Then $\psi(x) = e^{-2\pi i x_\infty}$,
  which is continuous.

  Note that as a additive group $\QQ$ is generated by $1$ and the numbers
  $1/ p ^ k, k \ge 1$. Then it suffices to check that for all prime number $p$ and positive $k$,
  $\psi(1 / p ^ k) = 1$. In fact,
  \[\psi(1 / p ^ k) = \psi_p(1/p^k) \psi_\infty(1 / p^k) =
  e^{2 \pi i /p^k} e^{-2 \pi i /p ^ k } = 1. \]

  (b) It follows from similar discussion above.

  (c) Recall that $\tr : \AAA_K \to \AAA_\QQ$ is defined componentwise by

  \[\tr(x)_p = \sum_{v \mid p} \tr_{K_v/ \QQ_p} (x_v), \quad  \tr(x)_\infty =
  \sum_{v \mid \infty} \tr_{K_v/ \RR} (x _v). \]

  For each $\psi_p$ is additive and there is only finitely many places $v$ lie over $p$. Then we have

  \[\psi (\tr_{K/ \QQ}(x)) = \prod_{p \le \infty} \psi_p(\sum_{v \mid p}(\tr_{K_v/ \QQ_p} (x_v))) =
  \prod_{p \le \infty} \prod_{v \mid p} \psi_p (\tr_{K_v/ \QQ_p}(x_v)) = \prod_{v} \psi_v(x_v) = \psi_K(x).
  \]

  By (a), we have that for all $y \in \QQ$, $\psi(y) = 1$ and for all $x \in K$,
  $\tr_{K/\QQ} (x) \in \QQ$, then $\psi_K(x) = \psi(\tr_{K/\QQ} (x)) = 1$.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[5]
  \label{ex:ch7-5}
  We shall now construct the standard character for the completion $K_v$ of
  $K = \FF_p(t)$ at any \emph{finite} place $v$.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Let $\pi(t)$ be an irreducible element of $\FF_p[t]$ of degree
      $d \ge 1$. Show that $K$ has a unique place $v$ such that the polynomial
      $\pi(t)$ generates the maximal ideal of
      $\gotho_v = \{\alpha \in K_v : \abs{\alpha}_v \le 1\}$. Show further
      that $q_v = \card(\gotho_v/\pi\gotho_v) = p^d$, and that
      the polynomials in $\FF_p[t]$ of degree less than $d$ in fact generate
      $\gotho_v/\pi\gotho_v$.
    % Note: as defined, K^{(v)} contains the constants, so
    % K^{(v)} \cap \gotho_v = F_p and the sum below is not direct; part (c),
    % where deg z < nd, implicitly uses the strict inequality |y|_\infty < 1.
    \item Given any place $v$ of $K$ (possibly infinite), let us define
      \[
        K^{(v)} = \{x \in K : \abs{x}_u \le 1 \ \forall u \neq v\}.
      \]
      Show that $K_v = K^{(v)} \oplus \gotho_v$. [\emph{Hint}: By replacing
      $t$ by $t^{-1}$ if necessary, one may assume that $v$ is defined by an
      irreducible polynomial $\pi(t) \in \FF_p[t]$ and then apply the previous
      part.]
    \item Let $v$ be any finite place of $K$; that is, one defined by an
      irreducible polynomial $\pi(t) \in \FF_p[t]$ of positive degree $d$.
      Show that every element $y$ of $K^{(v)}$ can be written as $z/\pi^n$
      for some $n \ge 0$ and polynomial $z = z(t) \in \FF_p[t]$ of the form
      \[
        c_1 t^{nd-1} + c_2 t^{nd-2} + \cdots + c_{nd}
      \]
      with $c_j \in \FF_p$ for all $j$, possibly all zero. If
      $x = y + \alpha \in K_v$ with $\alpha \in \gotho_v$, put
      \[
        \psi_v(x) = e^{2\pi i c_1/p}.
      \]
      (This is of course well-defined despite the status of $c_1$ as a residue
      class modulo the prime $p$.) Show that $\psi_v$ is a nontrivial
      character of $K_v$ of conductor $\gotho_v$.
    % \psi_\infty is the corrected character of Exercise 3(b); with the
    % book's e^{2\pi i a_1/p} the identity \psi_v(y)\psi_\infty(y) = 1 fails.
    % The book writes f for z in the hint.
    \item Let $v$, $\pi$, and $y$ be as in the previous part. Show that
      $y \equiv c_1 t^{-1} \pmod{t^{-2}}$. Conclude that
      $\psi_v(y)\psi_\infty(y) = 1$, where $\psi_\infty$ is as in part~(b) of
      \cref{ex:ch7-3}. [\emph{Hint}: Write $\pi$ as $t^d g$, with $g$ a
      polynomial in $t^{-1}$ of constant term $1$, so that
      $y = z g^{-n} t^{-nd}$.]
  \end{enumerate}
\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
  Let $A = \FF_p[t]$. We consider $\pi$ to be a monic polynomial.

  (a) Existence: take $v = v_\pi$.

  Uniqueness: suppose that $|\pi|_v < 1$.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Case (1): $|t|_v > 1$. For $f = \sum_{i= 0}^m a_i t^i$ with $a_m \neq 0$,
    the terms have distinct absolute values $|t|_v^i$. Therefore, $|f|_v = |t|_v^m$.
    and in particular $|\pi|_v = |t|_v^d > 1$. This is a contradiction.
    \item Case (2): $|t|_v \le 1$. By triangle inequality, $A \subseteq \gotho_v$,
    and $P = \{f \in A : |f|_v < 1\}$ is a proper prime ideal containing $\pi$.
    Since $(\pi)$ is maximal, then $P = (\pi)$. Write $f = \pi ^ k g$ with
    $\pi \nmid g$. Then $|g|_v = 1$, so $|f|_v = |\pi|_v ^ {\ord_\pi f}$.
    Hence, place $v$ is equivalent to $v_\pi$.
  \end{itemize}

  As we know that $\gotho_v/ \pi \gotho_v \cong A / (\pi)$. Then we have
  that $\card{\gotho_v / \pi \gotho_v} = p ^ d$.

  (b) Note that $K ^ {(v)} \cap \gotho_v = \{x \in K : |x|_u \le 1,  \forall u\}$.
  Such an $x$ does not have finite poles, then $x \in A$. And since $|x|_\infty \le 1$,
  then $\deg x \le 0$. Then $K^{(v)} \cap \gotho_v = \FF_p$, but not $0$.
  Therefore the sum $K_v=K^{(v)}+\mathfrak o_v$ is \textbf{not direct sum}.
  It can be written as a direct sum as follow,
  \[K_v=K^{(v)}_0\oplus\mathfrak o_v, \]
  where $K^{(v)}_0 = \{y \in K^{(v)}: |y|_\infty < 1 \}$ for $v$ finite, and
  $K^{(\infty)}_0 = t\FF_p[t]$. In both cases $K^{(v)}_0 \cap \FF_p = 0$, hence
  $K^{(v)}_0 \cap \gotho_v = 0$ and the sum is direct.

  Finite $v$. Let $x \in K_v$. Then $x$ can be written as
  \[x = \sum_{n \ge r} a_n \pi ^ n = \sum_{n = r}^{-1} a_n \pi^n
    + \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n \pi^n. \]
  Note that $\sum_{n = r}^{-1} a_n \pi^n \in K ^{(v)}_0$ and
  $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n \pi ^n \in \gotho_v$.

  Infinity $v$. Recall that $K_\infty = \FF_p \lseries{t^{-1}}$ and
  $x \in K_\infty$ can express as
  \[x = \sum_{n = -\infty}^{r} a_n t ^ n = \sum_{n = 1}^{r} a_n t^n
    + \sum_{n = -\infty}^{0} a_n t^n. \]

  And $\gotho_\infty = \FF_p \pseries{t^{-1}}$ and $K^{(\infty)} = \FF_p[t]$. Note that
  $\sum_{n = 1}^{r} a_n t^n \in t\FF_p[t] = K^{(\infty)}_0$ and
  $\sum_{n \le 0} a_n t^n \in \gotho_\infty$. Then we have that
  $K_\infty = K^{(\infty)}_0 \oplus \gotho_\infty$. (Again
  $K^{(\infty)} \cap \gotho_\infty = \FF_p$, so $K^{(\infty)} + \gotho_\infty$ is not direct.)

  (c) In this part of exercise, we will describe the form of the elements of $K^{(v)}$.

  Let $y \in K ^ {(v)}$ and write $y = a / b$ with $a, b$ coprime and $b$ monic.
  If there is another prime $\varpi \neq \pi$ divided $b$, then $|y|_\varpi > 1$, which
  contradicts to $y \in K ^ {(v)}$. So $b = \pi ^ n$. And since $|y|_\infty \le 1$, then
  $\deg a \le n d$. If $\deg a = n d$, then we can split off the constant as follows:
  \[y = c + (a - c\pi ^ n)/ \pi^n, \]
  where $c \in \FF_p \subseteq \gotho_v$ is the leading coefficient of $a$.

  According to above discuss, we have that $y \in K^{(v)}$ can be express as $z / \pi ^ n$
  with $\deg z < n d$. The coefficient $c_1$ does not depend on the choice of $n$:
  $z / \pi^n = z\pi / \pi^{n+1}$, and since $\pi$ is monic, the coefficient of
  $t^{(n+1)d - 1}$ in $z\pi$ equals the coefficient of $t^{nd - 1}$ in $z$. Together with
  the uniqueness of the decomposition $x = y + \alpha$ in (b), this shows that $\psi_v$ is
  well defined. Writing two elements of $K^{(v)}_0$ with the same $n$, we see that $c_1$ is
  additive, so $\psi_v$ is additive. Clearly $|\psi_v(x)| = 1$ for all $x$.
  Note that for $x \in \gotho_v$ we have that $y = 0$,
  then $\psi_v|_{\gotho_v} = 1$ i.e. $\psi_v$ is trivial on the open subgroup
  $\gotho_v$. Then $\psi_v$ is continuous. Hence, $\psi_v$ is a unitary character.

  Note that for $x = t^{d-1}/ \pi \in \pi^{-1} \gotho_v$, $\psi_v (x) = e ^ {2 \pi i / p} \neq 1$. Hence, the conductor of
  $\psi_v$ is $\gotho_v$.

  (d) Since $\pi$ is monic, $\pi = t ^ d g$, where $g = 1 + b_1 t ^{-1} + \cdots + b_d t ^ {-d}$.
  Hence, $g \in \FF_p\pseries{t^{-1}}^\times$ and $g ^ {-n} \equiv 1 \pmod{t^{-1}}$. Therefore,
  \[y=\frac{z}{\pi^n}=zt^{-nd}g^{-n}=(c_1t^{-1}+c_2t^{-2}+\dots+c_{nd}t^{-nd})
    (1+O(t^{-1}))\equiv c_1t^{-1}\pmod{t^{-2}}.\]

  By the definition of $\psi_\infty (y) = e ^ {-2 \pi i c_1 / p}$ in part~(b) of
  \cref{ex:ch7-3}, then we have that $\psi_v(y) \psi_\infty (y) = 1$.
\end{solution}
